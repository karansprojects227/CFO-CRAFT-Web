Where startup finance usually breaks

The problems rarely show up until a funding round or a board meeting forces them.

  • Runway calculated from the bank balance

    Burn is estimated from last month's outflow, ignoring payables, committed spend and working capital that grows with sales.

  • Investor updates rebuilt every month

    Each update is assembled from scratch, the numbers shift between versions, and investors notice.

  • Unit economics nobody has costed properly

    Contribution per order leaves out returns, discounts, fulfilment or channel fees, so growth looks cheaper than it is.

  • Diligence finds what the books did not

    Share capital filings, foreign investment reporting, ESOP accounting and old GST gaps surface during a round and delay the close.

Compliance points that come up in diligence

The ones most likely to delay a round.

  • Foreign investment reporting

    Share allotments to foreign investors must be reported to the Reserve Bank within prescribed timelines. Missed or late filings need regularisation before a new round can close cleanly.

    Reference: Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019 and RBI Master Direction on Foreign Investment in India

  • Share capital and ESOP records

    Allotments, ESOP grants and their filings with the Registrar of Companies need to reconcile with the cap table your investors hold.

    Reference: Sections 42 and 62, Companies Act, 2013

  • Payments to micro and small suppliers

    Amounts owed to micro and small enterprises must be paid within the agreed credit period, capped at 45 days. If a payment runs past that limit, the income-tax deduction for the expense is deferred to the year it is actually paid, so an overdue supplier ledger at year-end raises that year's taxable income. We track this in the monthly creditors review, not at year-end.

    Reference: Section 15, Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act, 2006; Section 37(2)(g), Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly Section 43B(h), Income-tax Act, 1961, for tax years up to 2025-26)

This is general information, not tax or legal advice. Applicability depends on your entity, turnover and facts, and is confirmed during the Finance Diagnostic.

Is this a fit?

A good fit if

  • You have raised, or are raising, a seed to Series A round.
  • You make, source or distribute physical products: D2C, consumer, hardware or food brands.
  • You have revenue and your investors expect monthly reporting.

Probably not yet if

  • You are pre-revenue and need a pitch-deck financial model only. That is specialist project work .
  • You need a full-time CFO in the office. We can help you hire one.
  • Your model is pure software with no inventory or supply chain. Firms that specialise in SaaS will serve you better.
